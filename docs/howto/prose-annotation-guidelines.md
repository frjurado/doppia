# Prose Annotation Guidelines

**Status:** adopted 2026-10-08 (Track E, stage E0). Revisit after the Caplin
graph review (E1) and once the review flags (E4, E6) have run over real prose.
**Applies to:** `fragment.prose_annotation`, the free-text commentary written
in the tagging tool.
**See also:** `../reports/editorial-acceleration-planning.md` § 4.7 (where this
policy was agreed), `../adr/ADR-041-editorial-machine-assistance-and-provenance.md`,
`../adr/ADR-006-internationalisation-strategy.md` (annotation translations),
`../adr/ADR-007-prose-annotation-vectorisation.md` (prose as the future
retrieval corpus), `../seed-drafts/cadences-design.md` § "Terminology and
prose conventions" (the conventions for concept *definitions*, which this
document extends to fragment commentary)

This document is written for two readers: the editor writing the commentary,
and the reviewer, human or machine, checking it. § 6 turns the policy into the
checklist the reviewer applies.

---

## 1. The principle

**The prose says what the structured fields cannot say.**

A fragment already states, in structured form, its concept, its property
values, its stages and their bounds, its capture-extension values, and (via
`movement_analysis`) the harmony in its range. All of that is queryable,
translatable by overlay, and kept consistent by validation. Prose that repeats
it adds nothing a reader cannot see in the sidebar. It also **goes stale**:
when a property is corrected, the sentence restating the old value silently
contradicts the record. Nothing flags the contradiction, because prose is not
validated against fields.

The commentary is also the highest-value content in the system (ADR-007): it
is what cannot be recovered from the score or regenerated by a pipeline. It
should be spent on what only the editor can provide.

---

## 2. What belongs in the prose

Three kinds of content, in rough order of importance.

### 2.1 The argument, where the label is not obvious

Why this label and not the neighbouring one. This is the most valuable sentence
a commentary can contain, and it is expected whenever the editor hesitated:
between IAC and PAC, between evaded and deceptive, between a half cadence and a
dominant arrival, between a way station and an independent cadence.

> *PAC rather than IAC: the 2 on the downbeat is an appoggiatura, and the
> soprano reaches 1 within the final tonic.*

> *Read as an evaded rather than a deceptive cadence: the bass leaps away on
> the expected arrival and the music restarts the cadential progression, so
> the substitute chord does not end the gesture.*

### 2.2 Relation to context

What the cadence closes, opens or defers, and how it relates to the cadences
and formal units around it. This is the part the structured fields capture
only partly (`CadenceFunction`, `PhraseClosure`, `ThemeClosure`, the
`fragment_pointer` extensions).

> *The way station anticipated by the half cadence two bars earlier; the
> stronger close arrives only with the repetition.*

> *First authentic cadence of the subordinate theme; the movement's earlier
> attempts at closure in the new key were all evaded.*

### 2.3 What is notable or pedagogically salient

What a teacher would point at: an unusual voicing, a striking delay, a
textbook-clear instance worth using as a first example, a passage where the
labels sit uncomfortably and that is therefore good for discussion.

> *A clean first example of the cadential 6-4: all four stages are clearly
> separated and the dominant is prolonged by the 6-4 alone.*

### Empty is acceptable

If none of the three applies, if the fragment is clear, typical and
unremarkable, **no prose is better than restating the fields**. An empty
commentary means "nothing beyond the record". A restated one is noise that can
go wrong later.

## 3. What does not belong

- **Morphology already in fields:** the cadence type, property values,
  elaborations, the soprano degree, whether a stage is present.
  *Not:* "PAC with cadential 6-4 and soprano on 1."
- **Harmony labels as an end in themselves.** The harmony panel already shows
  them. Cite a chord only when it carries the argument ("the IV6 that replaces
  the expected tonic").
- **Location as content.** "mm. 8–12" restates `bar_start`/`bar_end`. Bar
  numbers are fine when they point *elsewhere* (§ 4).
- **Modelling rationale or tool notes.** Why the graph models something one
  way belongs in `cadences-design.md` or an ADR, as it does for definitions.
  Problems with the tool belong in an issue, not in commentary that will be
  published.

---

## 4. Repeated and related passages

A cadence that recurs (exposition and recapitulation, a repeated phrase, a
variation) gets commentary on **what differs**, not a copy of its
counterpart's.

The **link** between the two passages lives outside the prose. Track E stage E5
models passage correspondence as data, and the interface will render the link.
Until then, use an **interim convention** that a script can find and convert
later:

> *cf. mm. 98–102 (recapitulation)*

That is: `cf.`, then the measure range as displayed, then an optional short
qualifier in parentheses. Use it for any reference to another specific
passage, not only to recurrences. When E5 lands, these references are migrated
to links, and the prose keeps only the sentence about the difference.

Do not paste the counterpart's commentary. Two copies of the same paragraph
drift apart the first time one is edited.

## 5. Language, terminology and style

- **Language.** Write in the fragment's annotation language (`fragment.language`,
  ADR-006). Translations are a separate record with their own status
  (`machine` → `reviewed` → `authoritative`). Do not write bilingual
  commentary.
- **Concept and value names are the graph's names, in the graph's
  capitalisation.**
  - English: as in the domain YAML ("Authentic Cadence", "Way Station",
    "Pre-dominant").
  - Spanish: as in `backend/seed/translations/es.yaml` ("Cadencia Evitada",
    "Estación de Paso", "Dominante Secundaria").

  A synonym in prose ("interrupted cadence", "cadencia de engaño") makes the
  text harder to search and to translate consistently. Use the graph's term.
- **Spelling conventions** follow `cadences-design.md` § "Terminology and
  prose conventions" (for example "Pre-dominant", capitalised; stages named by
  function in running prose).
- **Notation in running text:**
  - Roman numerals as in the harmony panel (V6/4, ii6, V/V);
  - scale degrees as plain numerals in context ("the soprano reaches 1"), or
    "scale degree 3" where ambiguous;
  - measures as "m. 8" / "mm. 8–12".
- **Length.** Usually one to four sentences. Longer is fine when the argument
  needs it. Length is not a quality.
- **Register.** Addressed to a reader who knows the concepts: analytical,
  plain, no hedging formulas. Where the reading is genuinely uncertain, say so
  once and say why.

## 6. Review checklist

Applied by the reviewer at approval time. From E4 (deterministic checks) and E6
(model critique), it is applied as **flags** on the review queue. Flags write
nothing (ADR-041 § 2c); the editor resolves them.

| # | Check | Kind | Severity |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Prose **contradicts** a field or a harmony event (names a different cadence type, soprano degree, function, chord) | content | high |
| P2 | Prose **restates** fields with nothing added (§ 3) | content | medium |
| P3 | Doubtful case **without an argument**: the fragment carries a consistency flag, a DCML disagreement, or a low-agreement concept pair, and the prose gives no reason for the label (§ 2.1) | content | medium |
| P4 | **Copied commentary**: identical or near-identical to another fragment's prose (§ 4) | content | medium |
| P5 | **Terminology**: a concept or value named by a synonym rather than the graph's term, in the annotation language (§ 5) | form | low |
| P6 | **Capitalisation/spelling** of graph terms inconsistent with the conventions (§ 5) | form | low |
| P7 | **Interim reference** not in the `cf. mm. …` form, so it cannot be migrated (§ 4) | form | low |
| P8 | Typos, grammar | form | low |

P5–P8 are mechanical. P1–P4 need judgement: P1 and P4 are partly checkable by
code, P2 and P3 belong to model critique. A flag is a prompt to look, not a
verdict; dismissing a flag is a legitimate resolution.
