# Concordance: cadence domain ↔ Caplin, *Cadence* (2024)

**Date:** 2026-10-08. Part of the [E1 review](README.md); statuses are
defined there. Page numbers are the book's printed pages. "Glossary" means
pp. 583–592. Proposals referenced as **P1**–**P9**, **Q1**… are in
[`proposals.md`](proposals.md).

Each row paraphrases the graph's claim (concept `definition`, schema
`description`), gives Caplin's position with pages, and a note. The graph text
quoted is the current YAML, not the design-draft "definition seed" lines
(`cadences-design.md` § "Terminology and prose conventions").

## 1. Framing concepts (no single graph node)

These are Caplin's working distinctions that the whole domain relies on. They
explain several rows below.

| Caplin concept | Pages | Where it lands in Doppia | Status | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A cadence must end *something*: a unit that began at the same level. A presentation never closes with a cadence, a basic idea only with one of limited scope, and nothing above the theme (exposition, movement) has a cadence. | § 2.2, pp. 15–20; § 2.7, p. 36 | Implicit in `CadenceFunction`, `PhraseClosure`, `ThemeClosure` | Consistent | The graph never lets a cadence close a section above the theme. |
| **Cadential arrival** (the moment the final harmony appears) vs **cadential function** (the span from the start of the cadential progression to the arrival). The onset can be retrospective. | § 2.5, pp. 31–33 | A cadence fragment is the cadential-function span; the arrival is the onset of the final tonic (AC) or of the dominant (HC). | Consistent | Worth stating in the tagging guide: fragment bounds = cadential function, not "the cadence chord". |
| **Content vs function.** Cadential content in a non-cadential position (a basic idea ending V–I) is not a cadence. | § 2.6, pp. 33–36 | Function as property (decision 5) | Consistent | Supports decision 5 directly. |
| **End vs stop.** The cadence is where the progression arrives, not the last event before a caesura. An HC needs a harmonic progression *into* the dominant; a dominant reached earlier and prolonged does not make a new HC. | § 2.9, pp. 46–48 | Not stated anywhere | Loose end | Tagging-guide rule: the HC arrival is the first arrival of the dominant through the progression. Candidate E4 check (fragment ending deep inside a prolonged V). |
| **Syntactic vs rhetorical strength.** Syntax gives three degrees only (HC < IAC < PAC); meter, texture, dynamics, duration are rhetorical and do not define form. | § 2.11, pp. 50–54 | The concept hierarchy encodes syntax; `Unison`, `Covered`, metric position are rhetorical | Consistent | The best single argument for **P2**: rhetorical features that are visible in the notes should be computed, not tagged. |
| **Progression types:** prolongational, sequential, cadential; context decides ambiguous cases (I–V–I; VI–II–V–I). | § 2.3, pp. 20–25 | Harmony lives in `movement_analysis`; no progression-type data | Loose end | Out of the cadence domain. Matters for E3: a harmony-derived stage 1 is a candidate, never a fact (p. 61). |
| **Deviation** as a category (deceptive, evaded, abandoned; dominant arrival for HC). | p. 150; glossary p. 585 | Subtypes of the authentic / half branches | Departure (recorded) | Hard-locked decision 1 dropped the tripartite split. Caplin's category is recoverable as "not `*Realised`". |

| `HalfCadence` (abstract): arrives on the dominant; includes Dominant Arrival as a deviation | HC; DA as the HC deviation | § 3.3, pp. 101–103; § 4.4, p. 187 | Consistent | |
| `HalfCadenceRealised`: dominant in root position, no seventh, reached through a cadential progression | Conditions: root-position consonant triad; the dominant ends a group; it is heard as *ultimate*, not penultimate (that is the line with the evaded cadence). | `ReopeningHalfCadence`: an HC that retrospectively undoes a preceding AC; `FOLLOWS AuthenticCadenceRealised` | **Reopened HC:** an AC (usually PAC, also IAC) *or a substitute deceptive cadence* whose final tonic moves at once to a root-position V (a simple I–V HC, usually on a weak beat); a matching consequent follows. A reopened HC can even act as a way station (Haydn Op. 55/1). | § 3.3.5.2, pp. 124–129; glossary p. 589 | Consistent, with a contradiction in the edge | The `FOLLOWS` target excludes the DC case Caplin includes (K458 has an inverted tonic first, p. 127). | `DominantArrival`: dissolves cadential closure "by inversion or addition of the seventh", or is reached by non-cadential means (prolongation, sequence) | Two conditions: (1) a **final** dominant (ending a cadential progression) carrying a seventh; (2) a **terminal** dominant, the last harmony of a prolongational or sequential progression, which may be inverted. Often enforced by a fermata or elongation. Caplin keeps DA as a deviation against Burstein's "HC on V⁷". | § 4.4, pp. 187–208; glossary p. 585 | **Contradiction** (wording) | "By inversion" belongs to condition 2, not to a cadential dominant.
